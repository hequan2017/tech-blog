This article truly deserves some deep reflection....

You've worked so hard and endured so much loneliness and pain — yet nobody can see that you've become any the more outstanding?

By Wu Man Lan Jiang

(01)

A while ago there was a workplace TV show whose guests were all executives from Fortune 500 companies.

A stiff, awkward contestant walked out, and the Fortune 500 executives shook their heads nonstop.

Before he had said a word, he was already flushed red with nerves, his body trembling uncontrollably. It took him a long while to calm down before he stammered through his self-introduction.

If he hadn't introduced himself it might have been fine; but the moment he did, the Fortune 500 executives' heads were already shaking like rattle-drums.

This contestant had majored in English and, after graduation, worked as an English teacher at a school. But he was introverted and tongue-tied, and simply could not control his students. So he was forced to go to South Africa and change careers into sales. But South Africa was lonely.

How lonely? He couldn't put it into words.

Couldn't put it into words, then — in any case, the kid had no presence in South Africa, so he came back. Bravely stepping onto the stage today, all he wanted was to land a sales job.

He only wanted to do sales; any other job left him cold.

The Fortune 500 executives asked: If you simply must do sales, is it because you have some advantage?

He answered: No advantages, only disadvantages — I'm not good at socializing, I don't like talking to people, and strangers make me nervous.

The Fortune 500 executives told him: From the moment you walked out, judging from your self-introduction and your performance, I'm certain you are not the least bit suited to sales. Perhaps you'd better...

The contestant shook his head flatly: No, I must do sales. It's my dream. I'll work hard, I'll work hard... Please give me a chance, and I'll give you a surprise in return...

— But we know that this kid's hard work will most likely come to nothing.

(02)

I have a coder friend who makes roughly thirty to fifty thousand a month, with an insufferably arrogant manner: whenever he looks at us, he refuses to look us in the eye, favoring us instead with a pitying, contemptuous glance — otherwise, he feels, he'd be betraying his own high IQ.

One friend even held him up to encourage his own child: See that uncle? Not a shred of real ability — all he can do is make money. When you grow up, go learn to code...

At that he would rein in his arrogance in sudden alarm and say, over and over: No, no, no. Before you set your child on my path, first find out whether the child is cut out for it. I've seen plenty in this industry — coders without the gift suffer miserably. Let me put it this way: something I knock out in ten minutes with a few lines, some people spend a whole week on, writing thousands of lines that are still riddled with bugs when they run. Coding takes a feel for it, a feel much like painting or calligraphy. If the talent falls short, dabbling is harmless enough, but if you mean to make a living at it, I'm afraid it would hold your child back.

He told us about kids who, simply because his income was high, studied fiercely and burned the midnight oil for years with nothing to show for it — in fact they kept sliding backward. It happens a lot.

He said that whenever he ran into this, he would gently warn the person: This line of work isn't for you — truly it isn't. Get up, switch off the machine, and think of something else.

We scolded him: You can't just slam the road shut in front of someone's child; you owe them a way out. You say this isn't for the kid — then help us puzzle out what would suit them better.

He stayed silent for a good while, then answered: Whoever is no good at this will be no good at anything else either.

No good at anything!

Because their minds are a muddle; they have not yet found the self.

(03)

The hottest movie of late is probably Zootopia.

It is really a live-action film with the humans swapped for animals — a superb inspirational picture. After watching it, what kept echoing in my head was the film's main tagline: In Zootopia, every animal has endless possibilities... endless possibilities... endless possibilities... possibilities...

Human society is nothing more than one vast Zootopia.

But does everyone here truly have endless possibilities?

— In reality, some people can't do just two kinds of things: this kind, and that kind. And they fall short in just two ways: this way, and that way. Yet these people are extremely, extremely hardworking; they toil and strain bitterly. But — they work so hard, endure so much loneliness and pain, and still no one can see that they've become the least bit outstanding.

Is the animated film conning us?

Or is it that we've overlooked something?

(04)

In Japan there was a little girl named Saito Rie.

This child's life would be enough to film a hundred and eighty TV drama series, and might still not all fit — she was the number-one girl delinquent of Japan's underworld, moving among mobsters and fighting her way to a fearsome name. The Yamaguchi-gumi, the Inukai-kai — at the sound of her name they would stick up a thumb and bark: Yoshi! Very, very good...

Why did the girl forsake the straight road and charge headlong into the gangs? Because... normal human society had no room for her.

She was born fairly healthy, but at one year and eight months a grave illness took away her hearing. She lived in a soundless world, and as she grew up she lost the power of speech. In middle school she could not communicate with her classmates, drifted off on her own, and went off to make a name for herself in the underworld.

Just as she was riding high, a mama-san took her in as one of her girls and made her a drinking-hostess ×××.

A customer comes in, sees that this ××× is pretty, and calls for her. They sit down to chat and — good grief — the girl cannot speak. So how are you supposed to chat?

No speech, no problem: Saito Rie offered up pen and paper.

So the customer takes the pen and writes: I'm so tired. It's all so bitter.

Saito Rie takes the pen and continues beneath it: Fear the bitterness, and it lasts a lifetime. Face it, and it lasts only half a lifetime.

Hey — the customer brightens. This girl's head is full of chicken soup for the soul! He keeps writing: Still, bitter is bitter. It really is hard going.

Saito Rie continues: 'Toil' and 'bliss' differ by a single stroke of the pen and a single step of the road — what we call 'toil' is precisely the journey toward 'bliss.'

With wit pouring from her pen, the girl delinquent won the customers over: whatever they grumbled about, she gave back a beautiful sentence. Overnight she became the rage of the capital's Ginza, transformed from underworld delinquent into a soul-soothing ××× specialist. Television stations came hot on the scent and really did shoot a long, long drama series, The Pen-Talking Hostess ×××, making her more famous still.

At twenty-five, she published her first book.

(05)

Of course, Saito Rie is only a special case. She is not the only child to lose the power of speech, and such children are not necessarily as lucky as she was.

But Saito Rie is not an isolated case either.

Tang Xinzi, a Chinese scholar living in Japan, once mentioned a girl named Fumi in her neighborhood, deaf in both ears from birth and unable to speak. Yet through unrelenting effort she eventually found her own place in society.

Guess what work she does?

— The very occupation the Fortune 500 executives were shaking their heads at so violently at the start of this article:

Sales!

With no power of speech, how does she sell?

Though she cannot speak, she outperforms every speaking colleague in her company. She is the firm's top saleswoman with the best results; her annual income today is 30 million yen — about 1.78 million yuan in RMB.

(06)

For children like Saito Rie and Fumi, this world really is a Zootopia — the very place where their dreams of who they might become come true.

— But even inside the movie Zootopia, the only one with a dream and the ability to realize it was the rabbit heroine Judy; her 376 brothers and sisters were all perfectly ordinary rabbits, with no dreams and no striving, who did nothing but lie on the ground and dutifully munch carrots.

And we all know perfectly well that even if those rabbits did strive, the final result would hardly be outstanding.

— Just like the young man mentioned at the start of this article: he yearns to do sales, but he will not necessarily become another Fumi. And as my coder friend said, a coder without the gift will never become that industry's Saito Rie.

(07)

Saito Rie could set Ginza ablaze because she had a gift: her words always struck people exactly right.

And Fumi became a top saleswoman because, in school, she met a good teacher.

The teacher told her that the look in your eyes and a smile can transcend language — a better way of reaching people than words.

Fumi's mouth cannot speak, but her eyes can, and her smile is more sincere and moves customers more deeply. She possesses an emotional intelligence higher than that of her speaking colleagues — that is the real secret of her high salary.

As for the kid the Fortune 500 executives turned down: what they rejected was not his effort, not his determination, but the fact that in this field he showed not the slightest capacity for growth.

To say it again: what everyone rejected was his capacity for growth!

Because he has not yet made himself ready.

(08)

Think back to the kid on the workplace show who wanted to do sales: from the moment he appeared, he showed how estranged he was from reality.

— First, he lacks a gentle, smiling face. Of course, you cannot blame him for that; most Chinese faces are set in deep gloom and grievance — a face like that will do fine for stalking the streets to slash someone. But the commercial world runs on gentle strength, which is proof enough that not just him but most of us, ourselves included, are not yet ready.

Why do so many of us end up with faces of deep gloom and grievance?

Look at this kid's record: he failed at teaching and went to South Africa — a foreign land, mind you. They say a thousand books are worth less than ××× miles of travel, precisely because foreign lands jolt your senses and your thinking.

For instance, a friend of mine also went to South Africa and came back saying, lonely, so lonely. How lonely? He said he kept a chicken in South Africa, and with nothing to do each day he would chase it around for fun — chasing and chasing, chasing and chasing — until at last, and guess what happened?

The chicken died of exhaustion.

Whenever the friend gets to this point, no one listening can keep from laughing — he can make the dullest thing on earth sound this entertaining. You know at once what a nimble brain the man has, how lively his thinking. A man like that is suited to practically any career.

The opposite case is this contestant on stage. He too went to South Africa, but all he says is that it was lonely — as to just how it was lonely, he cannot make head or tail of it himself. Come on: you did at least live in a place like that — the local customs and ways, the interesting things, you were a million percent bound to have run into them, seen them, or at least heard of them.

— Yet when he talks about South Africa, he feels nothing whatsoever.

It is simply that nothing he sees stirs any feeling in him; however fascinating, it leaves him unmoved. His mind is sealed shut so completely that even if the earth went off with a bang and blew apart this very moment, he would not feel a thing.

His brain is still developing. His mind is one blank fog.

A person who feels nothing for his surroundings, for the things at his side, is a frightening thing. Whatever people like this attempt, they cannot succeed.

And ninety-nine out of a hundred of them wear faces of deep gloom and grievance.

Because he does not like this world.

(09)

A person who has lost interest in life is necessarily a person without a goal in life.

People with a goal in life live in a state of high excitement; they cherish their own lives dearly, terrified of missing the least interesting thing, afraid of shortchanging themselves, afraid of being wronged.

People without a goal in life have scattered minds and blank brains, listless all day long, sighing as they walk. Such people want to enter the workplace too, and may even work very hard. But their effort exists only to cope with the pressure of survival. What boss isn't a crafty old hand? One glance tells him you are not a fellow traveler, and he will hardly waste his time idling away the hours with you.

People without a goal in life are mostly those whose sense of self is still immature. Remember: the self is an organic whole, and a life goal is its power source. In a person without a goal, the missing parts of the personality must be filled in by other people. But the abyss of the soul cannot be filled, no matter how many resources you pour in. That is why people without goals always feel lonely and suffer the torments of pain — if you do not even know why you are alive, life is bound to be a slow, painful ordeal.

In people without a life goal, the personality is still developing; only when he finds his goal will he suddenly pull himself together, and after that he will never again be in pain, never again lonely. Because he is living inside his own life, he always feels there is not enough time, with no spare heart for long sighs and laments over wind and moon.

— Just like the kid on the workplace show: if sales truly were his life goal, then he would know that softening his smile into something warm, and making himself into an infectiously interesting person, is the first step into that industry.

But he didn't.

So we know he is not yet ready.

Life does hold real suffering, but more of the bitterness, more of the hardship, is nothing but the passive struggling of people who have lost their goals, headless flies battering blindly this way and that, nothing but meaningless self-pity gazed at in one's own shadow.

No one can fit into this world before finding a life goal. Until then, all his effort, hardship, and pain serve merely to torment himself and plague everyone else. Unless — you find the life goal that truly belongs to you. Only then does the so-called loneliness and pain come to an end, because you have become a person this world accepts: a happy person, an interesting person, a person everyone trusts.
