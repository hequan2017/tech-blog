> **About this post**
>
> This post is my personal study summary of GB/T 22240-2008 *Information Security Technology — Classification Guide for Classified Protection of Information System Security* (《信息安全技术 信息系统安全等级保护定级指南》): it reviews the criteria for each of the five security protection levels, the two classification elements (the object infringed upon when damaged + the degree of damage to the object), and the classification matrix formed by the three degrees of damage, and summarizes the complete classification process — grade business information security and system service security separately first, and finally take the higher of the two as the security protection level of the grading object.

> **Technical notes**
>
> GB/T 22240-2008 has been superseded by GB/T 22240-2020 *Information Security Technology — Classification Guide for Classified Protection of Cybersecurity* (《信息安全技术 网络安全等级保护定级指南》, the MLPS 2.0 system, i.e. classified protection of cybersecurity): the new edition extends the grading object from "information systems" to "classified protection targets of cybersecurity" (including basic information networks, cloud computing platforms, the Internet of Things, etc.). The five-level framework and the two elements of "infringed object × degree of damage" are largely carried over, so the matrix and process in this post still serve as a foundation for understanding.

## 1. The Five Security Protection Levels

GB/T 22240-2008 *Information Security Technology — Classification Guide for Classified Protection of Information System Security* defines five classification levels:

- **Level 1**: if the information system is damaged, it causes **damage** to the **legitimate rights and interests of citizens, legal persons, and other organizations**, but does not harm national security, social order, or public interests.
- **Level 2**: if the information system is damaged, it causes **severe damage** to the **legitimate rights and interests of citizens, legal persons, and other organizations**, or **damages social order and public interests**, but does not harm national security.
- **Level 3**: if the information system is damaged, it causes **severe damage** to **social order and public interests**, or **damage** to **national security**.
- **Level 4**: if the information system is damaged, it causes **particularly severe damage** to **social order and public interests**, or **severe damage** to **national security**.
- **Level 5**: if the information system is damaged, it causes **particularly severe damage** to **national security**.

## 2. The Two Classification Elements

The **object infringed upon when the classified protection object is damaged**, and the **degree of damage** inflicted on that object.

The infringed object covers three aspects:

- The legitimate rights and interests of citizens, legal persons, and other organizations
- Social order and public interests
- National security

The degree of damage to the object falls into three grades:

- General damage
- Severe damage
- Particularly severe damage

## 3. The Classification Matrix

| Infringed object \ Degree of damage | General damage | Severe damage | Particularly severe damage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Legitimate rights and interests of citizens, legal persons, and other organizations | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 2 |
| Social order and public interests | Level 2 | Level 3 | Level 4 |
| National security | Level 3 | Level 4 | Level 5 |

Information system security covers two aspects: **business information security** and **system service security**.

## 4. The Classification Process

1. **Determine the grading object**: it has a single, clearly identified unit responsible for security, has the basic elements of an information system, and carries a single or relatively independent business application.
2. **Determine the object infringed upon when business information security is damaged**: first check whether national security is infringed, then social order and public interests, and finally the legitimate rights and interests of citizens, legal persons, and other organizations.
3. **Comprehensively assess the degree of damage to the object**: for the damage to the grading object, pick one of the three degrees of harm.
4. **Determine the business information security level**. Benchmarks for judgment:
   - If the infringed object is the legitimate rights and interests of citizens, legal persons, or other organizations, take the overall interests of the individual or the organization as the benchmark for judging the degree of infringement;
   - If the infringed object is social order, public interests, or national security, take the overall interests of the entire industry or the nation as the benchmark for judging the degree of infringement.
5. **Determine the object infringed upon when system service security is damaged**.
6. **Comprehensively assess the degree of damage to the object**.
7. **Determine the system service security level**.
8. **Determine the security protection level of the grading object**: for an information system as the grading object, its security protection level is **decided by the higher** of the business information security protection level and the system service security protection level.
